Here is the future organizational structure Jack Dorsey outlines in the interview, detailing each layer's responsibilities, evolving structural position, and the key insights explaining why human roles at that level must remain.

---

### 1. Agents / Intelligence Layer (The Core Foundation)

- **Position in Structure**: Sits at the center of the organization, acting as a shared "world model" or "mini-AGI" rather than a traditional management tier.
- **Responsibilities**: Ingests all company artifacts—Slack messages, emails, code pull requests, recorded meetings, and documentation—to create a fully legible, real-time intelligence system. It handles communication flow, baseline task execution, and data retrieval across the company.
- **Key Insight (Why it exists)**: Replaces traditional middle-management reporting chains as the primary channel for relaying information, eliminating lossy human communication up and down a hierarchy and enabling a radically flat company.

---

### 2. IC (Individual Contributor – Builder / Operator)

- **Position in Structure**: Comprises the largest portion of the human workforce.
- **Responsibilities**: Engineers, designers, salespeople, and product managers who directly use tools and agents to build products or run business operations. Because they are augmented by agents, a single IC can handle the breadth of work that previously required a team of ten.
- **Key Insight (Why this layer stays)**: AI can generate output, but humans are required for irreplaceable **durable human skills: judgment, taste, and creativity**.

---

### 3. DRI (Directly Responsible Individual)

- **Position in Structure**: Leads specific outcomes and projects without acting as permanent administrative line managers.
- **Responsibilities**: Owns customer outcomes, sets project strategy, understands product roadmaps, and assembles teams of ICs to execute solutions.
- **Key Insight (Why this layer stays)**: AI cannot take ultimate responsibility; this layer is required for human **ownership and accountability** when determining whether an initiative succeeds or fails.

---

### 4. Player Coach (Former Managers)

- **Position in Structure**: **Moves out of the traditional reporting hierarchy.** Instead of being a manager that ICs report to, coaching becomes an _assignment_ where experienced builders are temporarily assigned to support others.
- **Responsibilities**: Builds the capability and craft capacity of team members. Instead of managing administratively or telling people what to do, they teach by actively doing the work alongside ICs or DRIs.
- **Key Insight (Why this layer stays)**: Maintains human **coaching, empathy, soft skills, and capacity building**. Keeping them active as builders ensures they teach through practical mastery rather than abstract oversight.

---

### 5. C-Level / Leadership (CEO & Executives)

- **Position in Structure**: The top steering layer, aiming for extreme org depth reduction (getting down to 2–3 layers max, or having everyone connect directly through the intelligence layer).
- **Responsibilities**: Sets company culture, principles, and tone. Executive leaders are expected to fluidly adopt all three lower roles—operating as IC builders, strategic DRIs, and coaches.
- **Key Insight (Why this layer stays)**: Functions as the **ultimate alignment checkpoint**. Their core job is ensuring the AI intelligence layer and the ring of human operators stay strictly aligned with customer outcomes, brand values, and strategic judgment.
