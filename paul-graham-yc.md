# Paul Graham — Core Concepts from YC and the Essays

A succinct map of the recurring ideas across Paul Graham's Y Combinator career and essays, grouped by theme. Essay titles are noted where a concept comes from a specific piece.

## What a startup is

- **Startup = growth.** A startup is a company designed to grow very fast. Growth rate is the single metric that matters, and every decision should be judged by whether it increases it. (*Startup = Growth*)
- **Do things that don't scale.** Early on, recruit users by hand, delight them individually, and stay small and manual. Scalable processes come later. (*Do Things That Don't Scale*)
- **Make something people want.** YC's motto. Most failures come from building something nobody needs, not from execution problems. (*How to Start a Startup*, *Be Good*)

## Finding the idea

- **Live in the future, then build what's missing.** The best ideas come from noticing gaps you personally experience, not from brainstorming. (*How to Get Startup Ideas*)
- **Schlep blindness.** Founders unconsciously avoid tedious or unglamorous problems, which is exactly where the under-served opportunities are. (*Schlep Blindness*)
- **Ideas that look bad but are good.** The strongest ideas seem unpromising to most people, which is why they are still available. (*Black Swan Farming*, *How to Get Startup Ideas*)
- **Start with a small, intense market.** Better to have a few users who love you than many who are indifferent. Expand from a narrow beachhead. (*How to Get Startup Ideas*)
- **Organic vs. sitcom ideas.** Ideas that grow out of your own needs beat ideas invented to sound like a startup. (*How to Get Startup Ideas*)

## Building the company

- **Founders over everything.** Bet on determined, resourceful, technically capable people. Cofounders should be people you already know and trust. (*What We Look for in Founders*, *The 18 Mistakes That Kill Startups*)
- **Launch early and iterate.** Ship a minimal version quickly, then let real users shape it. Perfectionism before launch is a form of procrastination. (*The 18 Mistakes That Kill Startups*)
- **Talk to users constantly.** The founders' job is to be in a tight loop with customers and the product. Everything else is secondary. (*How to Start a Startup*, *Before the Startup*)
- **Ramen profitability.** Get to the point where revenue covers founders' basic living costs. This buys independence from investors. (*Ramen Profitable*)
- **Default alive or default dead.** Know whether you would survive on your current trajectory without more funding, and act accordingly. (*Default Alive or Default Dead?*)
- **Startups die of self-inflicted wounds.** Most deaths come from founders losing motivation, running out of money, or cofounder disputes, not from competitors. (*How Not to Die*, *The 18 Mistakes That Kill Startups*)
- **Avoid fake work.** Meetings, press, conferences, and fundraising feel productive but are not growth. (*How to Start a Startup*)

## Fundraising and investors

- **Raise as little as possible, as fast as possible.** Fundraising is a distraction. Get it over with and get back to building. (*How to Raise Money*, *The Hacker's Guide to Investors*)
- **Investors follow momentum.** Growth and traction are the only reliable persuasion. Pitching is about clarity, not salesmanship. (*How to Convince Investors*)
- **Be formidable and honest.** Investors sense confidence grounded in real understanding, and it cannot be faked. (*How to Convince Investors*)
- **The equity equation.** Give up a share of the company only if it makes the remaining share worth more. (*The Equity Equation*)

## Founder mindset

- **Relentlessly resourceful.** The defining quality of good founders. The opposite is "hapless." (*Relentlessly Resourceful*)
- **Maker's schedule vs. manager's schedule.** Creative work needs long uninterrupted blocks. Protect them. (*Maker's Schedule, Manager's Schedule*)
- **Naughtiness and disobedience.** Successful founders tend to be mildly rule-breaking, curious, and willing to look foolish. (*What We Look for in Founders*, *The Word "Hacker"*)
- **Don't start a startup to become a founder.** Start one because you want to solve a specific problem. Founding for its own sake produces sitcom ideas. (*Before the Startup*)
- **Keep your identity small.** Attaching your identity to ideas makes you worse at thinking about them. (*Keep Your Identity Small*)

## Broader worldview

- **Wealth is created, not divided.** The best way to get rich is to make something valuable, and startups compress a lifetime of work into a few years. (*How to Make Wealth*)
- **Superlinear returns.** Outcomes are driven by compounding and thresholds, so small early advantages become enormous. (*Superlinear Returns*)
- **Cities and environments matter.** Ambition is shaped by surroundings, which is part of why YC clustered founders in Silicon Valley. (*Cities and Ambition*)
- **Taste and craftsmanship.** Good design, like good code, is simple, honest, and made by people who care. (*Taste for Makers*, *Hackers and Painters*)
- **Do great work.** Pick something you are curious about, work hard on it, and aim for what the best people in the field would consider excellent. (*How to Do Great Work*)
- **Write to think.** Writing clearly is how you find out what you actually believe. (*Putting Ideas into Words*, *Write Simply*)
