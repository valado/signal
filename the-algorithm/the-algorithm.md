Elon Musk's five-step engineering algorithm is a sequential framework designed to maximize efficiency and avoid wasting effort by ensuring you do not optimize things that should not exist:

1. **Question requirements:** Challenge every design requirement to make them "less dumb". Crucially, every requirement must be attached to a **specific, named individual** rather than a department, allowing you to directly challenge its necessity.
2. **Delete, delete, delete:** Go ultra-hardcore on elimination. The guiding principle here is that **"the best part is no part, the best process is no process"**.
3. **Simplify and optimize:** This standard engineering work should **only** happen after you are absolutely certain the part must exist and its requirements are as clean as possible.
4. **Accelerate cycle time:** Work to make things go faster, but only after completing the first three steps. Speeding up something that should have been deleted is entirely counterproductive.
5. **Automate:** This is the final step of the process. Automating too early—before questioning requirements, deleting parts, and optimizing—is a common mistake that leads to severe production bottlenecks.

Musk notes that the most common error made by smart engineers is **optimizing something that shouldn't exist in the first place**, which is why these steps must be repeated rigorously in this exact order.
